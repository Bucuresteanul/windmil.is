# Public Project Pipeline

**Project:** windmil.is  
**Scope:** Publicly evidenced Icelandic onshore wind projects relevant to infrastructure market entry  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-10-08

This is a public screening document, not a confidential bid list.

It classifies projects by what the available evidence supports today. It does not imply that work packages are open, that windmil.is has a commercial relationship with a developer, or that any project will be built.

---

## Screening Logic

A project becomes strategically relevant only when several conditions align:

1. credible developer and active project
2. sufficient planning / environmental maturity
3. plausible grid path
4. civil / foundation / erection scope not already closed
5. qualification path that a specialist entrant can realistically satisfy
6. timing that justifies resource allocation
7. acceptable regulatory and social risk

**Large MW numbers alone are not enough.**

## Benchmark — Vaðalda Wind Farm

**Developer / owner:** Landsvirkjun  
**Published scale:** 28 turbines, 120 MW  
**State:** first phase operating; remaining build continues toward full completion by end-2027  
**Entry classification:** BENCHMARK / NOT PRIMARY TARGET

### Evidence

- Landsvirkjun commissioned the first phase on 15 September 2026.
- Enercon holds the principal turbine design, manufacture, transport, installation and testing package.
- Ístak holds the principal package for 28 reinforced-concrete turbine foundations, crane bases, groundwork and related civil works.
- Landsnet has commissioned the 220 kV Ferjufit connection infrastructure.

### Implication

Vaðalda validates the existence of utility-scale wind construction in Iceland.

It is not treated as the main greenfield entry target because the principal turbine and civil / foundation packages identified so far are already awarded.

## Priority Cluster — wpd Ísland / Langanesbyggð

### Brekknaheiði + Sauðanesháls

**Developer:** wpd Ísland  
**Published combined envelope:** up to 532.8 MW, 74 turbines  
**Public stage:** environmental-assessment planning active; framework-plan treatment still material  
**Entry classification:** HIGH STRATEGIC RELEVANCE / NOT BID-READY

wpd published an environmental-assessment plan for the two concepts in January 2026. The combined project concept is described as a phased 74-turbine development with an estimated total development and construction period of approximately 5–7 years.

Brekknaheiði alone is currently described by wpd as up to 338.4 MW with up to 47 turbines.

### Why it matters

- Very large civil and turbine-installation scope if projects progress.
- Development is active enough to justify monitoring.
- Work is still early enough that future contracting architecture may not be fixed publicly.
- Regulatory, environmental, grid and local-community gates remain substantial.

## Early Relationship Signal — Sauðanesháls Trial Project

**Developer:** Wpd wind Iceland North ehf.  
**Published scale:** up to 9.9 MW, 2 turbines  
**Public stage:** environmental assessment required  
**Entry classification:** SMALL PROJECT / STRATEGIC SIGNAL / REGULATORY GATED

In August 2026, Iceland's environmental and natural-resources appeals committee rejected wpd's request to overturn the decision requiring a full environmental assessment for the two-turbine trial project.

The ruling records:
- proposed turbines of approximately 150 m or 180 m maximum height
- unresolved environmental questions
- proximity to the much larger Brekknaheiði / Sauðanesháls plans
- the need for additional assessment

### Important legal uncertainty

Law no. 66/2026 now brings wind project concepts into the Framework Programme when installed capacity is at least 10 MW **or when the wind structure exceeds 30 metres at its highest point**.

Because this project is below 10 MW but far above 30 metres, its precise treatment under the amended framework should be confirmed before any timing assumption is made.

## Advanced Environmental Work — Sólheimar

**Developer:** Qair Iceland  
**Published current EIA scale:** 209 MW, 29 turbines up to 200 m  
**Public stage:** environmental-impact assessment opinion issued in February 2026; framework treatment remains unresolved  
**Entry classification:** ADVANCED DEVELOPMENT / HIGH GATING RISK

The official environmental-assessment database records the project and a completed assessment-opinion process.

The 5th-phase Framework Programme project board proposed Sólheimar for the **waiting category**, rather than immediate utilisation.

In September 2026, Húnaþing vestra reiterated concerns about local benefit, social licence and visual impacts from nearby wind proposals.

### Implication

Sólheimar is more advanced in environmental process than many early concepts, but development maturity does not equal procurement readiness.

## Nearer-Horizon Candidate — Garpsdalur

**Developer:** EM Orka, owned by Empower and Greenvolt  
**Current developer scale:** 88.2 MW, 21 turbines  
**Public stage:** advanced development, but Framework Programme release is not yet treated as resolved  
**Entry classification:** HIGH COMMERCIAL RELEVANCE / REGULATORY GATED

The fifth-phase project board proposed Garpsdalur for the **waiting category**.

In July 2025, the energy minister proposed moving Garpsdalur to the utilisation category. That proposal is a positive maturity signal, but it should not be confused with a final construction release.

By September 2026, Garpsdalur was still being considered in the sixth Framework Programme phase. Húnaþing vestra continued to identify white-tailed-eagle uncertainty as a reason not to move the project from waiting status until sufficient evidence exists.

Current Greenvolt Power material describes the project as 88.2 MW / 21 turbines.

### Why it moves up the watchlist

- active, identifiable developer group
- defined current project envelope
- substantial historical environmental work
- ministerial support for utilisation-category treatment has existed
- construction scale is directly relevant to foundations and civil works

### Grid position

EM Orka publishes a 132 kV connection concept to Geiradalur and states that a grid-integration study was conducted with Landsnet in 2019.

That is useful evidence of a plausible route, but it is not a current capacity commitment. Landsnet's current planning material documents regional Westfjords system-strength and voltage constraints.

**Public grid classification:** G1 — concept established; current connection conditions unresolved.

See [Grid Gate](./GRID-GATE.md).

### Why it is not bid-ready

- framework status still requires confirmation
- environmental / eagle issue remains material
- current grid capacity, reinforcement, timing and cost are not established
- no relevant open construction package has been established publicly
- contracting architecture for civil / foundation scope is not yet established here

## Status Requires Revalidation — Hróðnýjarstaðir / Storm 1

**Developer:** Storm Orka  
**Publicly discussed scale:** historical documents describe materially different envelopes over time, including 80–130 MW and later 264 MW  
**Entry classification:** REVALIDATE BEFORE PRIORITISING

The official environmental-assessment database contains an environmental report and related material.

Older Storm Orka material expected generation by 2026, but that milestone has not been established by current public evidence reviewed here. Older correspondence also raised transmission-connection timing concerns.

### Implication

Do not use historical schedules as a current pipeline forecast.

Before allocating attention, confirm:
- current project owner / developer
- current design envelope
- framework status
- grid path
- financing and procurement status

# Public Screening Result

## Highest-value developer signal

**wpd Ísland** currently represents the strongest public portfolio signal for long-term monitoring because it presents multiple active Icelandic wind concepts across several municipalities and has already advanced environmental work in Langanesbyggð.

This is **not** evidence of an available contract.

## Highest-value project signals

- Garpsdalur — nearer-horizon single-project candidate if regulatory release is achieved; no open relevant package established yet.
- Brekknaheiði / Sauðanesháls — very large active development cluster and strongest long-term portfolio signal, but still heavily gated.
- Sólheimar — advanced environmental process, but framework and social / environmental risk remain material.

## Near-term conclusion

No project identified in this public screen is yet evidenced as an immediately open, qualified foundations / erection tender for windmil.is.

That is the next information threshold that matters.
