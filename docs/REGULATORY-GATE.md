# Regulatory Gate — Icelandic Onshore Wind

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-10-08

This document captures only regulatory changes that materially affect project timing and market entry.

It is not legal advice.

---

## 1. Wind is now expressly inside the Framework Programme

Iceland adopted Law no. 66/2026 in June 2026.

The amended Framework Programme legislation expressly covers wind project concepts where:

- installed electrical capacity is **10 MW or more**, **or**
- a wind-energy structure exceeds **30 metres** at its highest point.

This is strategically important because project screening can no longer use a simple “below 10 MW” assumption to infer that a tall turbine project falls outside the framework.

**Primary source**
https://www.althingi.is/altext/157/s/1457.html

---

## 2. Certain protected areas are excluded

The amended legislation excludes wind treatment through the Framework Programme in specified protected-area categories, including:

- Ramsar areas
- UNESCO World Heritage areas and nominated areas
- areas in Part B of Iceland's natural heritage register

This makes early geospatial exclusion screening a high-value activity.

---

## 3. Municipal position is a formal decision factor

The project board must specifically consider whether the relevant municipality has expressed a formal position on the wind proposal and the reasons for that position.

Views of neighbouring municipalities may also matter where their interests are affected.

### Strategic implication

“Social licence” is not merely reputational.

Municipal position is now embedded directly in the statutory evaluation logic.

---

## 4. Grid location is a formal decision factor

The amended law requires specific consideration of whether a project:

- improves electricity security
- aligns with national transmission-development policy
- is near substations and transmission lines

### Strategic implication

Grid proximity and system usefulness should be screened before detailed commercial analysis.

A technically attractive wind resource with weak transmission logic may have lower decision value than its MW headline suggests.

---

## 5. Regional development matters

The law also requires attention to effects on employment and regional development, especially in areas facing demographic or regional-development challenges.

### Strategic implication

A market-entry proposition that can demonstrate credible local economic value may be materially stronger than a purely external-contractor proposition.

This does not establish any requirement for a specific local partner.

---

## 6. Landscape, wilderness and birds remain explicit gates

The framework assessment must consider, among other matters:

- internationally important bird areas
- migration routes and habitat
- nearby residents
- wilderness
- landscape and visual effects
- whether the site is already disturbed by existing infrastructure

The 2026 Sauðanesháls trial-project appeal shows that even a two-turbine proposal can face full environmental assessment where scale, location and uncertainty justify it.

**Primary source**
https://uua.is/urleits/uua2605011-saudaneshals-i-langanesbyggd/

---

## 7. Framework processing was separately reformed in 2026

Law no. 17/2026 introduced major changes to the broader Framework Programme process.

The government's summary states that the process is intended to operate more continuously rather than only through infrequent large cycles, with statutory time limits and a 25-year energy-supply strategy informing evaluation.

### Strategic implication

The historical reputation of the framework process as purely periodic should not be projected unchanged into future schedules.

However, faster procedure does **not** remove:
- environmental assessment
- municipal planning
- transmission constraints
- project finance
- procurement
- litigation or appeal risk

# Market-Entry Consequence

Before windmil.is treats a project as an actionable opportunity, the project should pass at least these public gates:

1. **Framework gate** — status and applicability understood.
2. **Municipal gate** — formal local position understood.
3. **Environmental gate** — assessment stage and material objections understood.
4. **Grid gate** — credible connection path and timing.
5. **Developer gate** — active owner / developer and current design confirmed.
6. **Procurement gate** — relevant package not already awarded.
7. **Qualification gate** — entry requirements realistically satisfiable.

Only then should detailed bid economics receive significant attention.
